## Which kind of US IP do you actually need?

Before price, the type, because picking wrong makes the cheap option expensive.

**Residential** IPs come from real consumer connections. They get the highest trust scores from anti-bot systems, which is why they're the default for scraping Amazon, Walmart, Target, and Google SERPs. They're billed per gigabyte and slower than datacenter IPs.

**Mobile** IPs sit behind carrier NAT, meaning thousands of real devices share one public address. Blocking that address means blocking paying customers, so anti-bot vendors mostly don't. This is the option for app-level data, mobile pricing, and sites with aggressive detection. It costs the most per GB because the supply is the scarcest.

**Datacenter** IPs come from server networks. Fast, cheap, and easy to fingerprint. Fine for open catalogs, uptime checks, and high-volume jobs where nobody's inspecting who you are.

**Static ISP** proxies sit somewhere in between: residential-grade addresses on stable, long-lived infrastructure. Good for account management where the IP can't move. Worth knowing that DataImpulse doesn't sell this category, so if stable dedicated US IPs per profile is your requirement, you'll need a second vendor.

## Sticky or rotating: the setting that decides whether your job works

DataImpulse supports both session types, and the choice is usually made by the target site rather than by preference.

**Rotating** gives you a fresh IP on every request. It's the right default for stateless work at volume: crawling product listings, collecting SERPs, monitoring prices across many US cities. Spreading requests across thousands of addresses is what keeps you under rate limits.

**Sticky** holds one IP for a defined window, configurable from 1 to 120 minutes, with 30 minutes as the default. Sticky connections are port-based, get assigned ports in the 10,000 to 20,000 range, and are held via a session tag in the proxy username. Use it for anything with state: logging in, multi-step checkout flows, forms, and account management where each profile needs a consistent identity.

Here's the honest caveat, and it came straight from DataImpulse support when a reviewer asked: the 120-minute figure is a configuration ceiling, not a promise. Residential IPs belong to real people whose devices go offline at any moment. When the underlying device disconnects, the session rotates to the next available IP automatically. Average real-world session length is closer to 30 minutes. If your workflow can't tolerate an IP changing mid-flow, you need a retry layer, or a different proxy category.
